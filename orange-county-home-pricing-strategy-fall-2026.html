How Aggressively Should You Price Your Orange County Home Right Now?

By Nick Smith · 2026-10
← Back to all posts

The difference between pricing right and pricing $50,000 too high isn't just time. It's negotiating position. And once a listing goes stale in Orange County, buyers can tell.

That's the market you're stepping into this fall.

Countywide Expected Market Time is sitting near 99 to 101 days as of late August 2026, and roughly one in five active listings have already been on the market longer than 90 days. Average days on market is running around 62. These aren't balanced-market numbers. They're numbers from a market where buyers have real leverage, and most of them know it.

Inventory hit a 2026 high this summer too, with somewhere between 4,874 and 5,054 active homes on the market against only about 1,494 to 1,535 pending sales, per FRED data from early September. That gap between what's listed and what's actually going under contract tells you a lot about where the power sits right now.

What happens once a listing goes stale?

Price reductions have climbed from roughly 55% to about 66% of active Orange County listings as of August 2026. That means two out of three sellers are already chasing the market down after opening too high. Every week a home sits, buyers start assuming something's wrong with it, even when there isn't, and the first price cut rarely recovers what a sharp opening number would have captured.

The sellers doing well right now are the ones opening at or just below market value, generating traffic in the first couple weeks, and closing inside a 30 to 40 day window. That's the range that keeps you, not the buyer, driving the negotiation. In practice, that looks like:

  • Getting a real comparative market analysis, not a Zestimate and not last year's comps. You want sales from the last 60 to 90 days in your specific city and price range.
  • Pricing to where buyers are actually searching, not to your target number. Buyers search in brackets, so a home listed at $1,025,000 misses everyone searching under a million.
  • Leaving room to negotiate without giving away money you didn't need to. That balance looks different for every home and every neighborhood.

Inventory usually tapers some as fall goes on, so waiting until October or November could mean fewer homes competing with yours. It also means fewer buyers out looking. Whether that trade works in your favor depends on your price point and how much time you actually have.

Your number depends on your home's condition, its location, and the exact week you list. That's where a current, local market analysis, not a generic estimate, makes the difference between a clean sale and a slow slide of price cuts.

Common questions I get about this

How aggressively do I need to price my home to sell in Orange County right now?

Right now the market favors buyers. Expected Market Time is running 99 to 101 days, and about two-thirds of active listings have already taken a price cut. Pricing at or close to true market value from day one, based on recent comps in your specific city and price range, gives you the best shot at selling in 30 to 40 days without losing ground at the negotiating table. Pricing high almost always costs more in the end than just pricing right from the start.

How long will my home actually sit on the market this fall?

As of early September, average days on market countywide is around 62, and Expected Market Time is closer to 100 days. About one in five listings have already crossed 90 days on market. Homes priced correctly from the start are consistently selling faster than that countywide average. The gap between the fast sales and the slow ones usually comes down to opening price.

Should I list now or wait for less competition?

Orange County inventory hit a 2026 high this summer, somewhere around 5,000 active listings, and it typically eases some heading into fall. Waiting means less competition, but it also means fewer buyers actively looking. Whether that trade makes sense depends on your price point, your home's condition, and your actual timeline. There's no single right answer here. The trade-off is real either way.

How many sellers are actually cutting their price?

About two-thirds of active Orange County listings had taken at least one price reduction as of August 2026, up from around 55% earlier in the year. That's buyers gaining leverage as rates stay elevated and inventory builds. If your plan is to hold firm on an above-market number, the current numbers suggest that's a riskier bet than it might feel.

What should I expect to give up in concessions if my home sits?

The longer a home sits, the more a seller typically ends up giving, whether that's on price, repairs, or closing costs. Buyers know when they have leverage. The best way to limit how much you give away is pricing it right before you list, so you're negotiating from strength instead of catching up.
