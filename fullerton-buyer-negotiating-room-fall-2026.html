Are Fullerton Buyers Getting Negotiating Room, or Are Good Homes Still Selling Fast?

By Nick Smith · 2026-11
← Back to all posts

If you read the headlines about Orange County this fall, you'd think buyers can name their price. In Fullerton, that's only half true.

The numbers do lean toward buyers. Countywide demand dropped to its lowest point since February, according to an October 6 market update. A report a week later showed Orange County inventory slipping below 5,000 homes while mortgage rates climbed to 7.40%. Plenty of buyers have stepped to the sidelines, and plenty of sellers have been sitting longer than they expected.

But the best-priced homes in Fullerton aren't waiting around. Realtor.com has Fullerton's sale-to-list ratio at 100% for September 2026, and some homes went under contract this October.

The split is sharper than most buyers expect. Homes that are priced accurately and show well are still pulling offers and closing fast. Homes that are overpriced, or that need work, are where sellers are willing to talk.

Where are Fullerton buyers actually winning concessions?

The October 13 county update said the average discount below list price widened for homes under $2.5 million as rates rose. That matters. A seller who priced optimistically six months ago is now a lot more open to a conversation about price, repairs or closing cost credits.

Market times are also stretching as the holidays get closer, according to the Fullerton Hills October digest. That gives you more room to do your homework and less pressure to waive contingencies.

In practice, it breaks down like this:

  • On a well-priced, move-in-ready listing, plan to offer competitively. Days on market can still be short.
  • On a home with deferred maintenance, a long stay on the market or a history of price cuts, you have a real opening to negotiate.
  • Closing cost credits are increasingly on the table. Ask what sellers in your price range are accepting right now.

That 100% sale-to-list figure is a median, so it hides a lot. Some buyers got thousands back in credits. Others paid over asking to land the home they wanted. Your position depends on the specific home, the specific seller and how you structure the offer.

Should you buy now or wait for more inventory?

Fall brings less competition, and that helps. It also brings fewer homes to choose from, which the Fullerton Hills digest points out too. That tradeoff is worth working through with someone who can pull current numbers for the price range you're shopping.

Common questions I get about this

Can I negotiate the price on a Fullerton home right now, or will I lose it?

It depends on the home. As of October 2026, Orange County demand has dropped to its lowest point since February, and market times in Fullerton are stretching as the holidays get closer. That helps you on homes that have been sitting. But well-priced, move-in-ready Fullerton homes are still going under contract quickly. Before deciding how hard to push, check the days on market and price history of the specific home.

Are sellers in Fullerton accepting offers below asking price this fall?

Some are. An October 13 Orange County market report said the average discount below list price widened for homes under $2.5 million as mortgage rates climbed to 7.40%. Realtor.com data also shows Fullerton's sale-to-list ratio at 100% in September 2026, so plenty of homes are still closing at asking. Sellers with overpriced or long-sitting listings are the most open to lower offers and closing cost credits.

Is now a good time to buy in Fullerton, or should I wait for more inventory?

Fall usually brings less competition than spring, and sellers who have been on the market a while tend to be more motivated. The tradeoff is that the holiday slowdown also means fewer homes to compare. If you find one that fits, current conditions can work in your favor. Waiting for a different inventory picture is a personal call that depends on your timeline and price range.

What mortgage rate should I expect as a Fullerton buyer right now?

An October 13 Orange County market update put rates at 7.40%. Your actual rate depends on your loan type, down payment, credit profile and lender, and it can change before you close. Get a current quote directly from a lender instead of planning around one number.

What does the escrow process look like when buying in Fullerton?

In California, escrow is handled by a neutral third party that coordinates the transfer of funds and title. The seller provides disclosures including the TDS, SPQ and NHD. Timelines, fees and transfer tax amounts vary by transaction, and your agent and escrow officer will walk you through the specifics once you are under contract.
